# Product

## Register

product

## Users

Knowledge workers who deal with large PDF documents as part of their job: paralegals organizing case files, accountants handling financial packets, office managers splitting board documents, HR staff distributing onboarding packets. They open this app intentionally to solve a specific task — not casually. The context is a desk workstation, mid-workday, often under mild time pressure.

## Product Purpose

A desktop utility to split one PDF into multiple named output files by visual page selection. Success means the user gets their output files in the right location, with the right names, without touching a command line or fighting a bloated office suite.

## Brand Personality

Professional, simple, trustworthy. The product is a workhorse — it earns trust by doing one thing well and staying out of the user's way. Voice is direct and jargon-free.

## Anti-references

Nothing that reads as youth-oriented, recreational, or startup-flashy. No bold gradient heroes, neon accents, or playful copy. The wrong direction is Figma, Notion, or Linear — those tools signal creative or developer work. The right direction is Microsoft Word or TurboTax: familiar, institutional, and confidence-inspiring.

## Design Principles

1. **Earn trust through calm.** The interface should feel like a steady colleague, not a product. Restrained color, consistent spacing, no visual noise.
2. **Tool, not destination.** Every UI decision should shorten time-to-output. Decorative elements have a high bar.
3. **Respect the workflow.** Users have an existing task (split this PDF). The app should insert itself minimally and get out of the way.
4. **Familiar vocabulary.** Visual patterns borrowed from office productivity software (Word, Acrobat) are a feature, not a limitation. Users recognize them immediately.
5. **One accent, used with purpose.** Color draws attention to actionable elements only. No decorative color.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG AA minimum. Color-blind accommodation: no information conveyed by color alone — group identification always includes a label. Reduced motion: respect `prefers-reduced-motion` where possible.
